# Stellar dApp Support Escalation Matrix

When something breaks in a Stellar dApp — a stuck transaction, a Horizon outage, a smart contract bug — the difference between a quick fix and a multi-hour incident is usually whether the team already agreed on who owns what, and how fast. This guide gives you a starting escalation matrix to adapt.

---

## Why Stellar dApps need this more than typical web apps

A few things make on-chain incidents different from a normal backend outage:

- **Some failures are unrecoverable by design.** A submitted transaction with an incorrect destination can't be "rolled back" the way a database write can.
- **Dependencies you don't control.** Horizon, Soroban RPC, and the network itself can degrade independently of your own infrastructure.
- **User-visible urgency is different.** "My funds are gone" reports need a different triage path than "the page is slow."

Because of this, your escalation matrix should separate **on-chain** incidents (where the blockchain state itself is involved) from **off-chain** incidents (your API, frontend, database) — they often need different people and different response postures.

---

## Severity levels

| Severity  | Definition                                             | Example                                                                       |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SEV-1** | User funds at risk, or a bug enables fund loss/theft   | A contract bug allows over-withdrawal; a signing flow leaks a secret key      |
| **SEV-2** | Core functionality broken for many users, no fund risk | Transactions fail to submit for all users; wallet connect is broken           |
| **SEV-3** | Degraded experience, workaround exists                 | Slow transaction confirmation; a secondary feature (e.g. price chart) is down |
| **SEV-4** | Cosmetic or low-impact issue                           | UI glitch, non-blocking console error                                         |

---

## Owner assignments

| Severity | Primary owner                        | Secondary / escalation                   | Notify                                             |
| -------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| SEV-1    | On-call engineer (contract/backend)  | Engineering lead + security contact      | Leadership, legal (if funds affected), status page |
| SEV-2    | On-call engineer                     | Team lead                                | Status page, support channel                       |
| SEV-3    | Assigned engineer (business hours)   | Team lead if unresolved in target window | Support channel                                    |
| SEV-4    | Backlog — triaged in normal planning | —                                        | —                                                  |

Adapt the specific roles to your org chart, but keep the principle: **SEV-1 always has a named security-aware escalation path**, not just "whoever is on call."

---

## Response time targets

| Severity | Acknowledge within | Mitigate/workaround within | Full resolution target |
| -------- | ------------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| SEV-1    | 15 minutes         | 1 hour                     | 24 hours               |
| SEV-2    | 30 minutes         | 4 hours                    | 3 business days        |
| SEV-3    | 4 business hours   | 2 business days            | Next release cycle     |
| SEV-4    | Next business day  | —                          | Backlog                |

"Mitigate" for a SEV-1 involving a contract bug often means **pausing the affected contract function** (if your contract has an admin-gated pause, per your governance design) rather than shipping a full fix — buy time safely before you ship a rushed patch to production.

## A small escalation template

Copy this into your incident channel when SEV-1/SEV-2 is declared:

```
INCIDENT: <one-line summary>
SEVERITY: SEV-<1-4>
DETECTED: <timestamp, UTC>
DETECTED BY: <person/monitor>
ON-CHAIN IMPACT: <yes/no — if yes, describe: contract address, affected tx types>
USER IMPACT: <who/how many, if known>
CURRENT STATUS: <investigating / mitigating / resolved>
OWNER: <name>
NEXT UPDATE BY: <timestamp>
```

Post an update at least as often as your "acknowledge within" target for that severity, even if the update is "still investigating."

## Verifying this guide renders correctly

Before relying on this as your team's actual runbook:

- Replace every role/name placeholder with your real on-call rotation and escalation contacts.
- Confirm your contract's pause/admin-gated functions (if any) actually exist and who holds the keys to trigger them — document that separately, since it's operationally critical and shouldn't live only in a markdown table.
- Render this page through your normal build (`pnpm build:content`) to confirm the tables and code block render as expected before publishing.
